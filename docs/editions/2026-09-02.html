Bhindi Masala, Burgers & Beer (or not…)
September 02, 2026 • Vol 1, Issue 31

the LEFTOVERS
🍜 Station Casinos, busy remaking the F&B footprint of all its properties, says both Lotus of Siam and Naxos Taverna are exiting Red Rock Resort, and that management of the popular Oyster Bar would move in-house. Lotus fans grumbled early-on that the resort version of their beloved Thai eatery did not compare to the original — complaints apparently validated by a 2023 lawsuit over the restaurant’s trademarked name and recipes.
​
🇮🇳 Is Indian food the new smashburger? It seems that way, as Las Vegas suddenly has more than a few Indian spots worth trying. The latest: Rangoli Social Eats recently opened in the previous Metro Pizza flagship, on E. Tropicana near UNLV. It’s a soft-open with a limited menu, but it’s worth noting that, even at full speed, the entire menu will be vegetarian and vegan only. Noted. Also? There is no alcohol available. Also noted.
​
🍺 That said, if you are in the neighborhood and crave a post-curry beer, the Irish Spot has the booze. Home of The Whale — a two-pound ‼️ fish (and chips) platter — the Irish replaced the legendary UNLV haunt Crown & Anchor, and has wisely nurtured its predecessor’s soccer-friendly Brit-pub vibe. The Irish was particularly rowdy fun during the World ⚽ Cup. Note: The Crown remains anchored on West Spring Mtn Rd.
​
🥗 A long drive from here, in a neighborhood far, far away… Stephanie Richter (an owner of Goodsprings’ legendary Pioneer Saloon) has opened Mama Tess’s Artisan Kitchen in Southern Highlands. According to the menu, the cafe-service eatery serves yummy-looking sandwiches, burgers, and salads, plus 12 different milkshakes. We’re not sure what might find us way out there, but we wouldn't hesitate to give it a try if-when.
​
🥨 Taking a bad idea cue from some Strip casinos, the Las Vegas Hofbräuhaus — a licensed replica of the 1589 Munich original — has put its Paradise Rd property up for sale for $16.2 million. Similar to the casinos, the Bavarian beer hall business is not for sale, just the property.
​
🍕 A slice of the ’90s is making a comeback: Mark Rich's NY Pizza & Pasta will soon reappear in the northwest valley. Rich landed in Vegas in the ’70s to run Paul Anka's Jubilation nightclub, ultimately trading the disco ball for a dough hook in 1994. (Both pizza joints closed a few years ago.) The quiet part out loud? NYP&P is taking over a storefront that was, until recently, one of Gio Mauro's Old School Pizzerias. Those ovens barely had time to cool.

🇫🇷​ The Michelin Guide Southwest came to Las Vegas (well, they held an awards ceremony here, at least…) — and we all know how John feels about it 🥱. If you haven’t seen the Las Vegas list yet, here you go… 

🍸​ The Bohemes have shifted some hours, which we suprisingly discovered when arriving for our semi-weekly 4pm meeting at Petite Boheme. Petite​ now ​opens at 5pm, and its happy hour has reversed to 11pm–2am nightly. Still need a fancy 4pm happy hour? That’s moved next door to Bar Boheme (the restaurant) until 6pm daily…

🔌 The former Gordon Biersch is getting a second life — but not as a brewpub. A remake will serve double-duty as a Vegas Loop station, and as office space for Musk’s Boring Company, which digs the Loop tunnels. Gordon Biersch opened to instant crowds in 1997 — when the Hughes Center was overflowing with leased offices — hosting a reliable happy hour packed with attorneys and cubicle refugees. It closed in 2023…

🍺​ Speaking of electric beer deals… ReBar has revived its rotating $3 blowout tap — available open-close, and just in time for 🏈 season…

ORDER OF THE WEEK
Mister B. Cheeseburger & Fries
Mae Daly’s Fine Steaks & Whiskeys
​
Smashburgers might own today’s burger-chat, but there was a time when a top-shelf hamburger was the steakhouse’s worst-kept secret. This was especially true at happy hour, when the bartop was where a pricey, proper meal became an affordable, social one. COVID stole that away, but competition — and the current economy, apparently — is bringing it back.
​
Above is Mae Daly’s Top 3 finisher in the Eat Talk Repeat sixteen-steakhouse burger crawl: eight ounces of ground Linz Heritage Angus, mild cheddar, lettuce, tomato, onion, and Mae’s Burger Sauce. It’s served at the bar only, and on the regular menu it's $35 (including fries 😜) and worth every dime. At Happy Hour (Tue–Thu, 5–7pm), the identical plate runs $12.
​
Twelve bucks 🤯. That's dive-bar grub money for a fat patty of quality beef, savored in a fancy room — with a (tip-only) valet just outside. Winning!
