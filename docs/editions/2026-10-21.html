the LEFTOVERS 🥡
☕ In this week’s edition of “Does the Arts District really need more…” the subject is coffee. And apparently, the answer is yes. Locally-grown Mothership Coffee is not only expanding its city-wide footprint, but is building a flagship café at downtown’s historic Mission Linen building. In a nod to the neighborhood, signage was recently painted (by hand!) by 50-year sign painter Bob Dewhurst. Mothership will soon join the Evel Knievel ​museum and a location of Napoli Pizza (unconfirmed) at Mission Linen.
— ✦ —
🥩 As mentioned on the podcast, Alex Prime is open at El Cortez, after an $8.5 million reimagining of Siegel’s 1941. A unique dual-concept eatery, it operates as Siegel’s for breakfast and lunch, shifting to the luxe “New York style steakhouse” Alex at 5pm. To accomplish this heavy lift, El Cortez consulted longtime F&B exec David Robins (Wolfgang Puck), and hired ​chef Joe Swan (Bar Boheme, High Steaks) to steer the kitchen. Named in tribute to casino owner Kenny Epstein’s late daughter Alexandra Epstein-Gudai, Alex is so fresh it’s not yet listed on the casino’s website, but you can get reservations here. Bonus: there’s an 8-seat bar for you social (or, solo) diners, and most classic cocktails are priced at a modest $12. 🍸
— ✦ —
🥟 Following up on last week's suggestion that buffets could make a Vegas comeback, Hakkasan has introduced another AYCE option (with a 2-hour limit…). Dim Sum After Dark​ replaces Hakkasan’s regular menu every Friday (5–10pm) for what feeeels like a reasonable $58 — the same endless dim sum deal available at Hakkasan during weekend brunch.
— ✦ —
🥂 Speaking of brunch… fans of Bryce Krausman and Dalton Wilson’s closed DW Bistro​ 😔 — where Sundays were an event — will want to book a November Sunday (10am–3pm) at Kim Owens’s Main St. Provisions. Enjoy DW faves (including crab benedict, chilaquiles, and of course, those scones), plus Bryce’s warm hospitality backed by a DJ (and… John’s out).
— ✦ —
🍳 Sticking with morning meals, ETR fave Winnie & Ethel’s now has a $9.99 breakfast special: two slices of bacon, two eggs (your way), and a pancake​ 😋. Available weekdays, 8–11am, you can think of it as a pre-work Happy Hour without the booze, and a good reason to check out W&E.
— ✦ —
🥂 And finally, another place John likely won’t go: the new Crysandra’s is coming to Boca Park. Aiming for a spring 2027 opening, the “elevated brunch” spot near Summerlin will also serve afternoon tea, cocktails, and dinner in a “glamorous, experience-driven atmosphere.” 🤔 While we always hope for the best, Boca Park hasn’t been kind to many eateries beyond Kona Grill and The Cheesecake Factory.

SPILLED TEA 🫖

🥩 On a recent episode of Eat Talk Repeat, Rebar and Davy's owner Derek Stonebarger said that his latest project, Arty's Steakhouse, was aiming for a Nevada Day opening. That date has again been pushed back as Arty’s awaits various agency approvals before it can open. Now looking at a post-holiday grand opening, Arty’s — certainly not the first restaurant in Commercial Center​ (Pat’s Chinese Kitchen, 1963?) — will likely be seen as a tangible kickoff to the center’s modern-day redevelopment.
— ✦ —

🍹 Boulder City Dispatch: One of our fave small-town escapes has been stepping up their F&B game for several years now, thanks in no small part to B.C. native and bar owner Grant Turner (Dillinger). Now, he has reportedly hired a mixologist from the Golden Tiki to shore up the offerings at his semi-secret tiki bar Cement Slipper. Accessible only via staircase from the alley behind Turner’s Skinny Bar, the Cement Slipper slings the goods every Tiki-Tuesday through Saturday.
— ✦ —

🥩 Both Las Vegas-area locations of Echo & Rig — chef Sam Marvin’s locally-born steakhouse and butcher shop, with another in Sacramento, CA — were unexpectedly closed on Monday. The Tivoli location’s glass door handles were chained and padlocked, and a sign indicated an unexpected maintenance issue had closed them for the night, while a sign at The District location simply said it would reopen Tuesday. Social media, as it does, immediately swirled with rumors about payroll checks bouncing, and the restaurant pushed back. We suggest calling your preferred Echo & Rig before going; as our resident (retired) attorney pointed out, a chain and padlock are suspicious for just a “maintenance issue”…
— ✦ —

🍷 DMs were also flying our way, saying Bank Atcharawan’s late night Chinatown Thai-and-wine eatery Jipata had closed. We confirmed directly with Atcharawan that Jipata is NOT closed. Digging in, we found Bank’s own social posts — one announcing his departure from the wine industry at the end of 2026, and another saying he was leaving Jipata. How much longer it stays open (or how long Bank will be there) is unknown. Atcharawan started as a sommellier at the renowned Lotus of Siam, and has since been behind several special places around the valley, including Lamaii (now operated by his brother, Bon). We’re forever fans of Atcharawan’s Thai bites and wine prowess, and hope to see him reemerge soon.
— ✦ —

ORDER OF THE WEEK
Ribeye? Flat Iron? Hangar?
The Black Sheep
​
Since its 2017 opening in the southwest, Top Chef Jamie Tran’s ​The Black Sheep has risen from neighborhood secret to one of our city’s must-visit restaurants. The name comes from Tran being the only sibling to follow her dad into the kitchen; that rule-breaking streak shows up on her menu, where imperial rolls and salmon skin tacos sit beside short ribs and vegan Vietnamese noodles.
​
Now, about that steak. Ash ordered it on a recent visit, and when we asked which cut it was, the full report was: “Damn good.” Our money’s on a hanger (judging by that beautiful grain), but it doesn’t matter much. Tran’s menu is small and seasonal, so the cut, the sauce, and the accompaniments change. On Ash’s night it was a rosy, sliced steak glossed with a deep, savory sauce over silky mashed potatoes and asparagus. On your night, it could be something else. But whatever’s on the plate, you’ll get something wonderful cooked by one of the most creative chefs in town.
