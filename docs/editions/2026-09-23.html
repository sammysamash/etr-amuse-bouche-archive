the LEFTOVERS
🥩 When the Tropicana (1957) was erased from the Strip for a baseball stadium, skepticism echoed through Las Vegas. Even as the site was cleared and graded, naysayers said, “Nay.” Now, as its skyline takes shape, an only-in-Vegas signpost has been announced for the stadium: a private steakhouse and lounge behind home plate. To gain access to the exclusive room, you’ll need season tickets — or a friend who has them. According to A's president Marc Badain, those ticket holders include “a lot of the people who have built 🤝 this town.” We would expect nothing less.
— ✦ —
🍣 Finally! A SoCal restaurant is heading to Las Vegas from somewhere other than LA or the OC. Glass Box — a Japanese restaurant and sushi bar from Del Mar — will open Saturday (Sep 26) at The Bend in the southwest valley. Glass Box offers sushi, sashimi, and omakase meals, plus that Las Vegas fave, an AYCE menu for lunch and dinner.
— ✦ —
🥢 The food hall replacing the other food hall at Uncommons is taking shape. Stix Asia recently revealed three respected born-in-Tokyo eateries as its first confirmed tenants: Ginza Bairin (1927), Ramen Bario (2010), and Nanamusubi ​(2016). All three are found in the Honolulu location of Stix Asia, but are new to the mainland. Nine additional restaurants are expected to be announced for the food hall, which should open this winter.
— ✦ —
🌮 Just a few days after being featured in Las Vegas Weekly, Carolina’s Mexican Kitchen​ suffered a kitchen fire. Luckily, nobody was hurt, but the fire closed the restaurant for a few days — and for independent restaurants, every day of sales counts. Carolina’s reopened Tuesday, so this is a good time to check out their scratch-made food and support a local business.
— ✦ —
🍷 Eight years. That's how long Michelin-recognized Partage has been quietly raising the French ante in Chinatown, a district better known for hand-pulled noodles than foie gras. To mark the anniversary, Chef Yuri Szarzewski has built a special seven-course dinner running two nights only (Sep. 23–24), and Nicolas Kalpokdjian has paired each course with wines from the 2018 vintage — the year the restaurant opened. Cheers!
— ✦ —
🎃 We know some of you are ready to leapfrog straight into Christmas, but slow your sleigh. Sure, Santa-styled pop-up bars are well established, but nobody should sleep on the Halloween transformations at The Haunting on Spring Mountain (Sand Dollar Lounge) and The Black Lagoon (Jammyland). The latter turns the Arts District’s reggae-and-rum bar into a full-blown creepfest, with immersive decor, Hollywood-quality lighting effects, and themed cocktails served in collectible glassware. Both launch Oct. 1, making them a fun, inexpensive way to kick off holiday season.

SPILLED TEA

🌮 While John will surely shrug his shoulders, other longtime Las Vegans will lament the loss of 61-year Mexican food institution Chapala’s. In November, Chapala’s will close its last location on East Tropicana Ave as the Flores family sets to retire. Opened first in 1965 (near Decatur and Washington), Chapala’s had several Las Vegas locations before closing all but the Tropicana spot. No, it won't win any Michelin awards, but Chapala’s is the kind of beloved family-run fixture that lends any city character.  
— ✦ —

☕ It’s been almost five months since Dig It! Coffee Co. relocated to Symphony Park and Almond & Oat called dibs on its old Arts District digs.​Work is finally being done in that corner of Casino Center Blvd, and a peek inside shows a full cosmetic overhaul underway — new floors, paint, countertops, and more will transform the corner into Kyle Cunningham’s third Almond & Oat. Notable: this will be the storefront’s third coffeehouse, and each one arrived with a particular Arts District hook. Golden Fog (2020–2022) was art focused and boasted a vegetarian-only menu; Dig It! employs adults of all abilities; and Almond & Oat pours plant-based milks only. Clearly, a plain old cup of joe need not apply.
— ✦ —

🐾 Speaking of coffee with a mission… Pups and Cups — a coffee trailer from 23-year-old entrepreneur Jordan Hass — donates a portion of profits to local animal-focused charities and posts photos of adorable, adoptable pups customers browse while waiting for their espresso. While Haas has grown her biz from a cart to a trailer, she’s aiming for a brick and mortar. Perhaps she should keep an eye on a specific corner of the Arts District…
— ✦ —

🌮​Aroma Latin Cocina’s Chef Kestler appears to be going all-out for Hispanic Heritage Month.​ From the way we read these two posts, Aroma ​is celebrating Hispanic Heritage Month twice — on Wednesday (Sep. 23) with an “eat what you want, pay what you want” party, then again, differently, on Thursday (Sep. 24) with a pay-what-you-want AYCE tacostranvaganza plus 2-for-1 beers 🍻. Chef Kestler, you may regret this. We won’t.
— ✦ —

👢 And… Friday is unofficially Dolly Parton Day (9/25… get it?), so a few Downtown spots are leaning in. Ada’s is pouring $9.25 specials all day, including the Dolly Spritz (“bright, pink, bubbly, pretty”) and select wines by the glass… The Dustland Bar is stacking Dolly music, drinks, and movies… And Yukon Pizza will have beer and wine specials, hush puppies and root beer floats, plus if you dress like Dolly, you get 15% off your tab. Friday would have been a perfect Dolly night, so Downtown is making one anyway. We expect more local spots to leap on the Dolly-wagon as word gets around.

ORDER OF THE WEEK
Traditional Classic Cinnamon Roll
Rebellion Market
​
In our modern world of viral obsessions, cinnamon rolls are having a moment. Not that they ever went away, of course. Between Cinnabon, Dolly Madison, and every neighborhood doughnut shop, some version of the cinnamon roll has always been nearby.
​
Most of us have our faves, but some of the best we’ve had lately come from the recently opened Rebellion Market in Henderson’s Anthem area. The market, a sister store to the neighboring Rebellion Pizza (also delish), sells fresh-baked loaves, amazing sandwiches made with said bread, and a variety of cinnamon rolls including Blueberry Streusel.
​
As you can see, we opted for a four-pack of the Classic, and it was the right call. Each roll pulls apart in soft, tender layers, with ribbons of buttery cinnamon sugar spiraling all the way to the gooey center. A layer of icing melts into every swirl, so each bite is warm, rich, and a little sticky. Good luck stopping at one. We couldn’t.
