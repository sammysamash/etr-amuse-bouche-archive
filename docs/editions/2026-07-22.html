Downtown Moves & Shakes + Durango Social Goes Vietnamese + Lots of Tea
July 22, 2026 • Vol 1, Issue 25

THE LEFTOVERS
​​🍸 Tireless James Beard semifinalist chef D.J. Flores is seemingly grinding 24/7 to leverage the success of Milpa (southwest LV) and Nocturno (DTLV). Yes, Flores has a second Milpa coming to Henderson, a new DTLV spot (Beautiful Stranger?) sliding someday into El Taco Rico (Main St. at Wyoming), and another, as-yet-unrevealed project in the works 😅.  We’re intrigued by his second Arts District cocktail bar — Cobra Clutch — licensed at the same Main St. address as the existing Houston's Hot Chicken. Flores confirmed with ETR that his bar director Lu Lopez is taking over the HHC bar, planning to open very quickly. We’re not sure how this setup will work once open, but we wouldn’t mind washing down spicy chicken sandos with amazing cocktails 🤞.
​
🧑‍🍳 Two respected local chefs are switching the code at Durango Social Club, shifting what was once a parade of various pop-ups (most recently, Lilli, coming soon to Chinatown) into a permanent restaurant. The name will remain Durango Social Club (undoubtedly confusing some and comforting others), but Dan Krohmer (Other Mama) and Crystina “Mama” Nguyen (District One) have collaborated on a new Vietnamese eatery to fill the spot. Nguyen created a small menu of standing dishes (Surf & Turf Vermicelli Bowl 😋) complemented by a changing daily specials board. She is aiming for “authentic Vietnamese flavors prepared with fresh, high-quality ingredients.” If the tastes are anything like District One, we’re all in.
​
🍕 Two other local chefs (well, pizzaioli) are also making news. John Arena, largely credited (along with partner Sam Facchini) with upgrading the Las Vegas pizza scene in 1980, recently shuttered two flagship Metro Pizza locations 😢. But that created opportunity for a new concept, Metro Sliceteria — the first slice-shop from the brand, now open at The Bend. Also open is Signora Pizza from Floriana Pastore. This comes after Pastore spent six years successfully slinging her amazing pies from what may be the only food truck operated by a World Pizza Champion. Her restaurant is great news for pizza fans — especially those who chased Pastore’s truck around town, hoping for a taste of her signature Neapolitan-style perfection. Saluti!
​
🍜 When The Sundry food hall opened at Uncommons in 2023, it landed with all the hype expected from a suburban mall reaching for urban cool. It swung big — 18,000SF, 15 purveyors, two terraces, an indoor-outdoor bar — but planted all that ambition in an unfinished development with almost nobody around yet to fill the seats. The vibe never clicked, either, annoying customers by requiring mobile or tablet-based ordering. After year of struggle, Sundry was done. Enter Stix Asia, a proven Asian-focused food hall from Hawaii, taking over this Fall. Promising 12 imported eateries plus a selection of Las Vegas favorites, Stix already has one big improvement going for it: humans take and deliver your order.
 

SPILLED TEA 

A byproduct of the Arts District’s rapidly rising success is even faster-rising rents. We’re told one Main St. hotspot (north of Charleston) is moving down the block to save 💰 after less than three years open. Despite the costs associated with relocation, ownership crunched the numbers after seeing new lease rates floated to them by their current landlord; moving was the smart choice. We’ve watched several Arts District retail stores move for better rent, but never an entire craft brewery 🍺 and raw bar 🦪…

A suburban cousin to a spot that helped jump-start the East Fremont rennaissance recently raised a reader’s eyebrows by charging a “large party fee” for a table of four. Given that their downtown eatery (with a different name) was initially centered around the “social dining experience,” we believe such a charge would never fly under Chef Kerry’s leadership. Just because it’s in your name doesn’t mean you have be an actual thief…

An Arts District tavern notable for its live music brunches (and, for fighting City Hall over just about everything) found itself caught up in a scandal regarding one of its performers. Apparently, the musician — who had performed with his sister during brunches at the tavern for several years — was discovered to have a sex offense conviction in 2020. Concerned patrons went on the offensive with a change.org petition — ultimately pushing the tavern to cut its ties with the duo…

A fancy French eatery in the Arts District is doing something rare in the restaurant world: briefly closing so the staff can enjoy un repos bien mérité — a well-deserved rest. Sometime in August, Bar Boheme (and Petite Boheme) will close for a week 😴 but when they reopen, magic happens. Bar Boheme will be open 7 days a week, with happy hour every day. And, Petite Boheme will add a late night happy hour (9pm–close?) serving select food from the Bar Boheme kitchen. The trade-off? We’d guess the Mon–Tue In Limbo Burgers pop-up will once again be in limbo…

ORDER OF THE WEEK
24-Hour Wagyu Pastrami
Hayworth​
​
When chef Alex Reznik (Top Chef alum, Beat Bobby Flay champion) opened Hayworth in February, Henderson foodies salivated. Finally — an adventurous, chef-driven restaurant in the 'hood! Think seriously elevated deli food, an Eastern European–influenced​ menu where live-fire proteins (chicken, filet, branzino) share space with smoked meats, Beef Tongue Agnolotti, Scallop Crudo, chicken-schmaltz-this and duck-fat-that.
​
Our pick? The 24-Hour Wagyu Pastrami. It’s exactly what it says — a day in the making, then crusted to order over live fire and served with circus beets, Brussels sprouts, and sunchokes. The plate works because everything subtly complements everything else: smoky, tender pastrami, sweet, light beets, and earthy, crunchy veggies.
​
Of note: Hayworth's celebrated happy hour is offering 50% off all drinks and happy hour food (4–5:30pm, bar and patio seating only). Enjoy cocktails, scallops, pirozhki, and more. The insider secret? An almost-requisite, off-menu burger is available — at the bartop only!
